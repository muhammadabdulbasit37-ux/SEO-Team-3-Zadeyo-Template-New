# Internal linking workflow

Required by [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) for every conversion and for affected content whenever articles are added. Integrate with the [SEO research workflow](seo-research-workflow.md) and [native content workflow](../content-workflow.md). This procedure is backed by the local graph checks described below; this document is not a completed site audit. Preserve the design, four products, checkout behavior and owner-supplied community content.

## Integration with the root conversion sequence

The ten-step sequence in root AGENTS.md is authoritative. During initial inspection, audit existing URLs, indexability, canonicals, locales, redirects and links; retain a dated baseline where output exists and label missing/stale evidence. Steps 1–3 research reader journeys and reconcile keyword ownership. In step 4, plan hubs, topical relationships and site-wide links, including useful connections from older articles to new pages. Record source, destination, benefit, anchor/placement, locale and existing-link status.

Implement reviewed links through native content in steps 5–6, preserving supplied quotations and pack/manifest contracts. Complete technical SEO and locale routing in step 7. In step 8, analyze current production HTML across the full route inventory, including pages unreachable by seeded crawls. A preliminary build is necessary when output is absent or stale; it does not replace final verification. Compare actual/planned edges and separate contextual links from navigation.

Run the root verification commands in step 9. Step 10 resolves orphan/disconnected groups, weak commercial/hub connections and other confirmed defects, then repeats affected builds, graph analysis and validators against the final state before reporting. A conversion is incomplete until generated output has been checked; unavailable tooling is a limitation, not a pass. Do not treat these responsibilities as a second competing phase list.

## Link selection and placement

Seek the maximum useful contextual coverage the writing naturally supports. For substantial guides, investigate about **10–20 useful contextual links**, and more where length/topic coverage warrants. Shorter pages may need fewer. Never pad, fail a build for fewer than ten, or count repeated navigation/duplicate destinations as satisfying that target.

Use semantic relevance and search intent across content types. An ESP article may connect to the main product, radar/features, installation, compatibility or troubleshooting when each helps its reader. A matching word alone is insufficient. Prioritize the homepage, main cheats product, HWID Spoofer, Skin Changer and UGC according to the reader's actual need; do not link every paragraph or article to every product.

Link supporting pages to appropriate hubs, closely related supporting pages to each other, and hubs/products back to useful guides or FAQs. Evaluate each direction independently; a useful link in one direction does not require reciprocity. Make the homepage a clear entry to commercial and informational sections while preserving existing layout and navigation.

Place especially useful links near the beginning when the discussion supports them, then throughout the body and useful closing sections. Do not force introductory links or clutter heroes, pricing, plan selectors or checkout. Consider existing links and hierarchy before repeating a destination; repeated functional CTAs/navigation are not automatically defects.

Use accurate, descriptive anchors: product names, natural partial keywords, feature terminology, branded navigation or clear sentence fragments. Prefer meaningful wording over generic “click here,” but preserve a natural sentence. Do not impose exact-match ratios, manufacture synonyms or promise unsupported features in anchors. Image-only links need meaningful accessible names.

Every new article requires outbound opportunity review and a review of relevant older pages for inbound opportunities. Update native related-guide/product mappings where useful, but widgets alone do not replace contextual links. Community submissions remain untouched: add only relevant website-controlled navigation, breadcrumbs, category/discussion suggestions or related resources around them. Never use unrelated threads/reviews as artificial link sources.

## Reusable local analysis

Inspect the actual implementation before extending it. Current reusable pieces are `src/config/validation.ts` and generated `.astro/validation-config.json` for route/locale/indexability policy, `scripts/html.mjs` (`parse5`) for HTML parsing, `scripts/validate.mjs` for target/fragment/redirect/SEO checks, and `scripts/content-workflow.mjs` for planned links and article inbound relationships. The validator now integrates `scripts/internal-links.mjs` when `internalLinks.enabled` is set in source validation configuration. Use installed dependencies; do not invent a command or claim graph coverage from ordinary validation alone.

Run `npm run build` then `npm run validate` to generate `.astro/internal-links.json` with source fingerprint, per-page counts, edges, findings and review candidates. Validation fails on orphan/unreachable indexable pages and unintended cross-locale anchors; existing checks fail on broken links/fragments and redirect chains. Underlinked pages, generic/repeated contextual anchors, query variants and noncanonical link forms produce review warnings. The report covers configured routes and all generated HTML, so unreachable pages cannot disappear from the inventory. Keep raw graph/recommendation artifacts under ignored `.astro/` and summarized evidence in docs. Prefer existing Node/parser tooling and local text matching before new dependencies or external embedding services. The installed `internal-link-optimizer` skill may supplement semantic suggestions where applicable; its crawl cannot establish completeness of unreachable routes, and its recommendations do not replace independent directional/reciprocal review.

- **Route inventory:** reconcile configured intended routes, generated HTML and sitemap. Do not discover the inventory solely by following links: that misses orphans. Explicitly list missing/extra routes, exclusions and analysis failures. Exclude checkout redirects, error documents, noindex utilities/legal routes and assets from indexable coverage targets, not from functional link checks.
- **Edges:** extract actual `<a href>` elements from final HTML and resolve relative, root-relative and same-origin absolute URLs with the configured origin. Retain source/destination, raw href, fragment/query, anchor/accessibility text, locale, placement and rel attributes. Classify main-content/contextual links separately from header/footer, breadcrumbs, widgets and language switchers; do not count every link inside `<main>` as contextual. Report classification uncertainty.
- **Normalization:** use routing helpers and configured redirects; map equivalent document destinations for counting while retaining original invalid/redirecting hrefs as findings. Validate fragments separately. Exclude self-fragments from inter-page counts. Identify unexpected old-template origins without silently treating them as current internal URLs; preserve legitimate historical/provenance/partner references.
- **Counts and discovery:** report unique referring source pages and unique outgoing destinations per URL, plus contextual counts and repeated occurrences separately. Exclude self-links from inbound coverage. Require every intended indexable page to have a crawlable incoming link from another discoverable indexable page and to be reachable from its locale homepage. Check reachability, because a disconnected cycle can have incoming links without being discoverable. Language-switch/hreflang connections alone do not establish useful same-locale integration.
- **Opportunities:** rank underlinked commercial pages and semantically related unlinked pairs using main text, headings, reader need, product facts and link deficits. Report any algorithm/model, inputs, limits and confidence. Simple lexical similarity is a candidate signal, not proof of semantic relevance. Assess a missing reverse direction even when the forward edge exists. Suggest a source passage, accurate anchor and reader benefit; never auto-inject links or use global text replacement.
- **Quality findings:** detect missing/unsafe/empty destinations, fragment errors, old domains, accidental query variants, redirect chains/loops, incorrect locale, redundant contextual links and missing planned hub connections. Inspect generic, misleading or keyword-stuffed anchors. Do not classify legitimate breadcrumbs, repeated CTAs or language switching as contextual spam.

## Locale, checkout and validation policy

Use `localizePath` and existing path/locale helpers for English, Spanish, German and French. Link to the available localized canonical destination; do not route translated readers to English by accident. Validate against actual routes, canonical and hreflang clusters. Record necessary language exceptions explicitly. Preserve original-language supplied discussions and intentional locale switches.

Use real HTML anchors with valid hrefs rather than JavaScript-only handlers for important links. Prefer direct canonical pages over unintended redirects, broken/deprecated domains or purposeless query variants. Do not use noindex/utility routes to distribute contextual SEO links. Legitimate legal links and purchase links remain functional exceptions: preserve unprefixed `/go/`, referral destinations, query/plan parameters and required rel attributes. Never follow checkout into partner requests for an audit.

Treat broken destinations, invalid fragments, unintended locale mistakes and orphan/unreachable intended indexable pages as defects to resolve before completion. Review underlinking, duplicate-contextual-link warnings, anchor quality and suggested counts semantically; do not impose blanket numeric failure thresholds. Record justified exceptions and fix real defects rather than weaken checks. When extending analysis code, use focused fixtures for meaningful graph cases such as unreachable cycles, locale/checkout exceptions and repeated template links.

## Implementation limits and review

The graph is offline and does not contact checkout or remote sites. Unique same-locale referring pages determine coverage, excluding self-links, language switchers and nofollow edges. Locale homepage traversal detects disconnected cycles. Products and locale homepages with no contextual incoming links are flagged for review; one-source pages are also flagged. There is no minimum article-link failure threshold.

Placement uses conservative HTML ancestor heuristics, separating navigation, related/archive wrappers and paragraph/list links. Review classification in the edge report; it is not a universal content classifier. Lexical candidates cover all same-locale existing/new pages and each missing direction independently, use local Unicode token overlap, and are capped at five per source. Scores are not semantic, authority or ranking measurements. No links are automatically inserted. Review candidates for reader benefit, choose an actual passage/anchor and update native sources plus the reviewed pack. Supplied forum/review quotations remain untouched; use website-controlled surrounding resources only.

Legitimate language links use existing `hreflang`/switcher markup. Any additional intentional cross-locale destination must have an exact `{source, target, reason}` in source `internalLinks.localeExceptions`; do not add broad exemptions for mistakes. Checkout and nonindexable legal/utility links remain functional but do not satisfy indexable coverage. Current source configuration enables the graph; reusable validator fixtures may explicitly omit it to isolate other checks. Missing target/fragment, forbidden-domain and sitemap checks remain owned by the existing validator, not duplicated in the graph module.
